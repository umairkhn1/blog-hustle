How to Write a Blog Post That Ranks and Gets Read to the End

Outline from the search results, answer in the first eighty words, then edit 15 percent out. A repeatable process for useful articles.

DH
Daniel Hayes Writing Coach
August 18, 2026 9 min read

Almost every blogging problem is an editing problem. The research is usually fine, the knowledge is usually real, and the draft still fails because nobody cut it. This is the process I use and teach.

Step 1: Build the outline from the results page

Search your target keyword and open the top five results. Write down every heading they use. Do not read the articles properly yet, just collect the structure.

You will see a pattern: three to six headings appear in almost every result. Those are the questions the reader expects answered, and leaving them out is why a well-written post fails to rank. Now list what none of them cover. That gap, plus a clearer answer to the common headings, is your article.

Step 2: Answer the question in the first eighty words

Before any preamble, give the answer. If someone asked "how much does a website cost," the first paragraph says the range and the main variables. Then explain.

This is not only for search engines. It is for the large share of readers who will never scroll, and it earns the rest of the article the benefit of the doubt. The standard habits that kill openings: "In today's fast-paced digital world," a paragraph about how the topic has become important, and a story about a client that starts before the reader knows what the article is about.

Step 3: Write headings as questions your reader asks

"Cost breakdown" is a label. "What does each part actually cost?" is a heading that promises an answer. Question-style headings also match how people search, which helps more than any keyword-stuffing trick.

Keep each section to one idea. If a section needs two sub-ideas, it should be two sections. Long sections are where readers leave.

Step 4: Use specifics instead of adjectives

Compare:

  • "This saves you a lot of time."
  • "This cut the formatting step from forty minutes to four."

The second sentence is worth reading because it can be checked. Specific numbers, actual screenshots, real prices, and named tools are what separate a useful article from a plausible one. If you do not have real numbers, get them before you publish.

Step 5: Edit 15 percent out

Print the draft or read it on a narrow screen so it feels different from the screen you wrote on. Then cut:

  • Every sentence that only restates the heading.
  • Adverbs holding up weak verbs: "very important" becomes "critical" or gets deleted.
  • The first sentence of most paragraphs, which is usually a warm-up.
  • Any paragraph that exists to fill a heading you added for symmetry.

Most drafts shrink by 100 to 300 words and get noticeably better. The information is unchanged; the reader just reaches it sooner.

Read it aloud once. It is the fastest test for sentences that are technically correct but impossible to follow. If you run out of breath, so does the reader.

Step 6: Make it easy to scan

Most readers skim first and read second. Give them a structure that survives skimming: short paragraphs of two to four sentences, bold on the key phrase in a paragraph (not whole sentences), at least one list in every long article, and images that show something rather than decorate something.

Step 7: Add the internal links while you still remember

Before publishing, add two to four links to related posts on your own site, and go back to the two most related existing posts and link them to the new one. New posts need incoming internal links or they sit unvisited.

Step 8: Schedule the update before you publish

Put a reminder in your calendar for six months out. Reopen the post, check the facts and prices, add anything you have learned, and improve the opening. Updated posts regularly outperform new ones, and they cost an hour instead of a day.

That is the whole system: outline from the competition, answer immediately, be specific, then cut. None of it is clever. All of it is repeatable, which is the point.
