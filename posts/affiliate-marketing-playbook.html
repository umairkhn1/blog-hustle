The 90-Day Affiliate Playbook for a Brand New Blog

A month-by-month plan for going from zero affiliate income to a modest, honest, repeatable commission stream. Includes what to disclose and what not to write.

RC
Ryan Cole Monetization Writer
September 2, 2026 11 min read

Affiliate marketing has a bad reputation because of the way some people do it: thin review pages, invented pros and cons, and links everywhere. Done properly it is the most reader-aligned income model available to a small publisher, because you only get paid when you genuinely helped someone choose.

Here is a realistic ninety-day plan.

Days 1 to 30: choose two programs and build one hub

Two programs, not twenty. Sign up for one tool you already use and pay for, and one program in a category your readers already buy. Read the commission structure in full, including cookie windows and whether commissions are recurring.

Then build one hub page. This is a substantial article that answers the core question of your niche: for a writing blog, "the tools a freelance writer actually needs." Not "top 50 tools." A specific, justified list of eight.

Every affiliate article you write for the next few months links back to this hub, and the hub links out to each of them. That structure tells search engines the pages belong together and keeps readers moving instead of leaving.

Days 31 to 60: write comparison and alternative pages

These convert better than reviews because the reader is close to a decision.

  • X versus Y: honest, feature-by-feature, ending with "choose X if... choose Y if..."
  • Best X for [specific person]: the specificity is the whole value. "Best laptops for design students under $900" beats "best laptops."
  • X alternatives: readers who searched for alternatives are already unhappy with the incumbent, so this is the warmest traffic you will get.
  • Cheaper ways to do X: often converts to a lower-priced product, and builds trust faster than a hard sell.

Publish three to five of these, each at least 1,200 words with real screenshots and real numbers. Update them monthly.

Days 61 to 90: capture emails and refresh

Add one relevant lead magnet to the hub page: a comparison sheet, a checklist, a short buying guide. Anyone who downloads it is a reader who is seriously considering a purchase, and email lets you reach them again when you publish the next comparison.

Then do a refresh pass. Check every affiliate link, confirm the pricing you quoted is still accurate, add a table of contents to long pages, and update the published date only if you actually changed the substance.

What you must disclose

In most jurisdictions, including the United States, you must clearly disclose affiliate relationships. Put a short plain-language line above the first affiliate link, not buried in the footer. Something like: "Some links below are affiliate links. If you buy through them, I may earn a commission at no extra cost to you."

Do it because it is required, and also because disclosure measurably does not hurt conversion. Readers are skeptical of unlabeled recommendations, not labeled ones.

The one rule that protects your site. Never recommend something you have not used. Readers can detect a page written from a product page in about four seconds, and search engines reward content that demonstrates first-hand use.

What kills affiliate sites

  • Thin reviews. A page that summarizes a product's own marketing copy adds nothing. Include what the product is bad at.
  • Too many links. If every noun is a link, nothing feels recommended. Three well-chosen links beat thirty.
  • Broken links. A dead affiliate link loses the commission and the trust. Check monthly.
  • Ignoring the reader's budget. Always include one cheaper option, even if it pays less.
  • Never updating. Prices and features change. Stale reviews quietly lose rankings and sales.

The numbers to track

Only four, in a simple sheet, once a month:

MetricWhy it matters
Clicks to affiliate linksTells you whether the page convinces people to act
Conversion rate per programTells you whether the product fits your audience
Revenue per 1,000 visitorsThe single number that benchmarks your whole site
Top three earning pagesWhere to spend your next hour of editing

Expect the first commission to arrive between day 60 and day 150, and expect it to be small. The point of the first ninety days is not the money. It is proving that a page you wrote can produce a sale, because after that it is a volume problem: more pages, better pages, same process.
